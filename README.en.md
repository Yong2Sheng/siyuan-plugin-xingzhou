# Xingzhou · Personal Action & Life System

Xingzhou is a self-contained SiYuan system for personal action and daily rhythm. Projects and transactions live in one top-level module; research-life balance, wellbeing, recovery, and nutrition intake live in another. All use plugin-managed private data without requiring extra SiYuan databases or documents.

> Current version: `5.0.0` (adds **lights-out plan adherence**: tap **Lights out now** in Bedtime preparation to record the real clock time on the spot, then answer one question the next morning — "Did I follow last night's lights-out plan?" When a plan exists and a measured time was recorded the verdict is derived automatically (15-minute tolerance; going to bed early always counts as met). Only "plan recorded but no measured time" asks once, and a missed verdict requires one sentence of reason. The morning no longer asks you to recall the actual lights-out time, and a night without a number is no longer a night without data. The verdict is computed at read time and never stored, so existing data needs no migration. Also adds an optional **watch sleep-onset time** in the same group as the watch score and sleep duration, which derives how long it took to fall asleep and is **recorded only, never judged** — lights out is what you control; falling asleep is not. 4.1.0: Action Details became structured fields plus an item-level to-do list. 4.0.0: the boundary reminder panel)

## Highlights

- Separate top-level Projects & Tasks and Life Rhythm modules, each with its own second-level navigation. Projects & Tasks uses All, Week, Review, and Relationship Graph without a separate Inbox page or global unclassified-capture button.
- Life Rhythm includes a standalone Daily Checklist with the same workday, Saturday, and Sunday reminders as the paper edition, without any fields to fill in. It switches between a screen-friendly Xingzhou view and a paper-like view, and both share the same check state. Each entry is labelled on the left with one of four type colours — Plain, Rhythm cue, Preparation, Boundary — with a legend under Today's progress (a "?" popover on narrow screens) and the same palette in the paper view;
- Checklist time nodes, titles, and reminder text are editable, and reminders can be added or removed **inline** within an entry ("＋ Add a reminder" expands an input in place and saves on Enter; hovering a row reveals "×", which turns into "Confirm delete" on the first tap and deletes on the second). Every reminder supports Pending, Completed, Partial, and Missed outcomes with partial counting as half. Display preferences, templates, daily results, and weekend training choices are stored per date in a separate `checklist.json` and survive restarts or synced devices;
- **Boundary reminders**: give any reminder a time of day (the dashed chip at the left of the row, next to the state selector) and it surfaces at the top of Today's Record as its time approaches. Overdue items and items due within 60 minutes are highlighted and sorted first; later items are listed outright and can be confirmed early, while anything further out is only counted and moves to the front when its time comes. "○ Done" writes the day state and tapping again undoes it, sharing one copy of data with the Checklist view. No system notifications and no modal dialogs, and the window advances on its own once a minute. **Cards are headlined by the actual item rather than the phase name** ("fish oil with the meal", not "Lunch"): the split happens at the first semicolon, the first clause becomes the title and the rest a supporting line, so a confirmed card still shows which item changed;
- A boundary time is **bound to the reminder, not to a row number**: inserting a row above it, deleting one, reordering, or rewording the text never moves the time onto another task, and only deleting the reminder itself removes it. Each row reads "time → state → reminder text", so the time sits in the same line of sight as the reminder it annotates;
- Nutrition intake totals calories, protein, carbohydrates, and fat by day without meal categories, with reusable one-tap food templates, proportional calculation from an actual weight or volume, and optional calorie/protein goals. Goal settings and template editing use separate cards, side by side on wide panes and stacked on tablets or narrow panes.
- Nutrition entries preserve the template values captured at logging time. Editing or deleting a food template affects only future entries and never rewrites intake history.
- Daily profiles for research workdays, Conference Day, Saturday reset, Sunday half-day research, and holidays. Conference Day covers conferences, talks, and collaborative meetings, waits until the event ends before asking about optional personal tasks, and skips nutrition logging; other profiles retain weekday defaults and per-date overrides.
- Daily forms follow a Morning / After lunch / Clock-out / After work / 21:00 timeline and save automatically. Stage buttons distinguish not started, incomplete, complete, and no-check-needed states; the missing-fields review jumps directly to each omitted key field while preserving non-linear entry and keeping ordinary notes optional.
- Professional study after lunch first asks whether study is planned and shows book, topic, plan, and completion fields only when needed. Watch sleep data (duration, score, onset) first asks whether the day has watch data, and only then reveals that group; weight supports kg/lb. **Last night's lights-out is now a plan plus a verdict instead of a recalled time**: tap **Lights out now** in Bedtime preparation to record the real time on the spot (written into that day's record, so it belongs to that night), and the next morning opens with a read-only review bar (last night's plan, the verdict, the measured time). Only "plan recorded but no measured time" asks once — "Did I follow last night's lights-out plan?" — a missed verdict requires one sentence of reason, and a night with no plan is recorded as "not applicable" instead of sitting in the to-fill list. The verdict is derived from measured minus planned at read time (15-minute tolerance; early always counts as met) and is never stored, so existing data needs no migration. **The watch sleep-onset time is recorded only and never judged**, and it derives how long it took to fall asleep (midnight crossing handled: lights out 23:45, asleep 00:40 = 55 minutes). The section uses one, two or three columns (620px, 1100px) with every control capped at 320px, and the sleep-window summary in Trends reports plan adherence.
- Training details appear only after training is marked complete. The after-work closure flow distinguishes Pending, Not needed, and Needed, then asks whether a next step exists before showing its text field. Not-needed durations remain not applicable rather than becoming zero.
- The 21:00 review first asks whether work continued after hours and whether there was an anomaly or observation. Explanatory fields appear only for Yes, so No no longer requires typing placeholders such as “none.”
- The personal-task note now asks Yes/No first: the text box appears only for Yes, and choosing No collapses it while keeping what was written, so switching back to Yes restores it. Notes already stored by earlier versions are recognized as Yes, so the new decision never hides existing text.
- The “first action when work starts tomorrow” note from the evening review appears automatically as a read-only hint in the next morning's Today Plan — reference only, never editable, and hidden when the previous evening left it empty.
- Holiday records treat work metrics as not applicable instead of failed or zero-valued.
- Transactions can be divided into a target number of execution slices with a per-slice estimate and independently scheduled on a calendar. Completion is shown as a percentage; missed and abandoned attempts remain in history while releasing a replacement slot. The calendar context menu completes a future slice early, completes today's slice, or corrects a missed past slice while preserving the planned date. A transaction holds at most 366 slices (noted on the field), and the slice card uses a compact one-line header with its note inside an ⓘ hint; the scheduling note and the Complete / Abandon this slice actions merge into the "0% transaction progress + progress bar" row when the card is wide enough, sit on the row directly beneath it on narrower cards while staying on the same line as the note, and the buttons stay a compact 22px tall. With a slice scheduled today the card simply reads "1 slice scheduled today". Clicking a completed cell or using its context menu **undoes that completion**: today returns to Scheduled, a past date to Missed, and a transaction you had already marked Done falls back to In Progress, while abandoned slices stay read-only. Today's cell is marked with 今 on the same line as the date, so the marker costs no extra height. Filling every target slice does **not** end the transaction by itself: the calendar only asks whether the transaction is done too, and it changes state when you press Complete transaction.
- The execution slice calendar and Week both show how much is still "To do" on a day (the total length of slices not finished yet). When everything committed for that day is done or finished early, the cell shows a green "To do 0 min" and turns green, making it easy to schedule other transactions on that day; the "Total X min · N slices" figure (scheduled + completed) stays beside it for contrast, the calendar header adds a summary row with the wording legend, and cells adapt their wording to the available width without ever leaving a bare number.
- A deadline acts only as the latest schedulable date. Transactions without a deadline can still schedule and move slices from today onward.
- Existing execution slices no longer prevent clearing a deadline or returning it to Pending; a new concrete deadline still cannot precede an existing slice.
- Week uses execution slices as dated work. An early-completed slice stays on its planned date and also creates a separate achievement on the actual completion date without adding scheduled workload. Life Rhythm automatically shows today's personal slices and can add a slice from an in-progress transaction with immediate synchronization back to Projects & Tasks. Each row can be completed, abandoned, or have the day's arrangement cancelled (cancelling removes only the slice, never the transaction), and a row whose transaction already ended stays visible with a Transaction done / Transaction cancelled badge while all three actions are disabled.
- Complete hierarchy browsing for areas, top-level projects, subprojects, transactions, and ideas (the retired "task" type is no longer offered; the hierarchy is Area → Project → Transaction/Idea). A blue Today badge appears only on Transactions that still have unfinished slices today, and each Transaction shows whether slices are not configured, still need scheduling, fully scheduled, or completed. Siblings can be manually reordered with per-row Up/Down controls or the drag handle.
- The filter group offers Today / All / Active projects / Future / Closed, with **Today first and remembered across restarts**: it keeps only transactions that still have unfinished execution slices today, and a matching transaction keeps its full ancestor path as context. Cancelling a transaction's slice for today while in Today does **not** switch you to another transaction — the transaction and its ancestor path stay in place, the detail pane and calendar never change items, and it is marked as moved out of today; scheduling the slice back onto today restores it, and it leaves the view only when you actively select another item or change the filter.
- Week remains compatible with legacy planned-date and per-day completion records. Transactions configured with execution slices now follow their explicit slice dates instead of being repeated mechanically across every day from start to deadline.
- Returning to Projects & Tasks, or reopening Xingzhou, restores the previous page, filters, hierarchy expansion, selected work item, and scroll position. When the previous transaction is completed or hidden, the selection falls back deterministically to the transaction with the most unfinished slices today (ties resolved by tree order), otherwise to the first visible transaction in tree order. The Today view is the exception: a selection that merely stops matching the filter is kept visible instead of triggering the fallback.
- New work items are created directly from the typed add buttons in All, the add-child action, or hierarchy context menus. Review separately lists Focused long-term areas and Ongoing top-level projects to distinguish what matters now from what is actively being done, then checks dates, action details, and weekly results. Legacy Inbox-status items remain readable and editable in All, but the current UI no longer creates new Inbox items.
- Direct editing of lifecycle, hierarchy, dates, execution cost, and action details; Current Action Details and Next Action render basic SiYuan Markdown while being viewed. **Clicking an action card opens a large editor window** (about 82% × 82% of the screen) for long text: the card and page stay put, so clicking a line inside the window starts editing on that line. Lists continue automatically, and sibling numbering is normalised in place after typing, deletion, cutting, or pasting (only the numbers are rewritten, and the browser's native undo keeps working). Save with the button or ⌘/Ctrl+Enter, cancel with the button or Esc, and **clicking outside saves**.
- Current Action Details supports images: paste a screenshot or drop an image file on the card. Images are stored in the SiYuan asset library at original resolution while the action text keeps only an `assets/...` path reference, so image bytes never enter the plugin data. Identical content is stored once; the editor window shows thumbnails (uploading / failed / size / per-image removal), and read mode caps image height and opens the original on click.
- Images in Current Action Details can be **copied from a context menu**: right-click a thumbnail, an image in the read-mode text, or the enlarged original in the preview dialog to copy the original image or the asset path. Copy original image writes the **original file bytes** from the asset library (a PNG is never decoded or resampled, so size and pixels match what you pasted, ready for pixel-level comparison in an AI conversation instead of a fresh, lower-resolution screenshot); non-PNG images are transcoded to PNG at their original size first, and the confirmation reports size and pixel dimensions. An unavailable clipboard or an unreadable asset explains itself rather than failing silently.
- When an item ends (Done / Failed / Cancelled / Abandoned), the images referenced by it and all its descendants enter a 7-day cleanup grace period: text and images stay untouched, and the item shows an "image cleanup · N days left" badge. The toolbar "Image cleanup" entry (with a pending-count badge) opens a dedicated page listing pending items and thumbnails; "Clean up now" opens an in-page step to select items and individual images. Cleanup deletes only files SiYuan considers unreferenced, marks and keeps images still used by documents, databases, or other unfinished items, and copies deleted files into SiYuan history first so they can be recovered. The same page includes Storage Health with image counts, unfinished usage, pending usage, whole-library size, and a per-item usage ranking.
- Cross-project hard prerequisites and should-stay-ahead relationships, with cycle prevention. New dependency pickers show only Ready and In Progress transaction candidates.
- Relationship Graph automatically shows every unfinished project and transaction with its long-term-area context and directional hierarchy, hard-prerequisite, and should-stay-ahead links. It supports search, filters, zoom, canvas panning, related-node highlighting, and opening details from the inspector.
- Optional links to SiYuan documents without requiring a document for every work item.
- **Trend view** (Life Rhythm › Trend) turns the numeric fields of the daily and nutrition records into line charts. Thirteen metrics are available; three are shown by default (Sleep window, Morning weight, Daytime energy) and the rest can be ticked on. **Sleep window** draws lights-off, wake-up, and planned lights-off as three lines of the same night (the plan is dashed), so "planned 23:00 → actual 01:00" is readable at a glance. **24-hour notation** keeps times monotonic across midnight (01:00 is plotted as 25.0), so averaging 23:30 and 00:30 gives 00:00 rather than 12:00. **Night anchoring** keeps an after-midnight lights-off in the night it belongs to (X is the evening the night started; the same morning's weight stays on its record date one column later, as the footnote says). **Bands instead of verdicts**: the late-night danger zone covers only 24:00–05:00 — colouring everything after midnight would paint the 06:00–08:00 wake-up target red too — while 06:00–08:00 has its own green band. Scores get bands from their own direction: energy, efficiency, and sleep quality are green at 4–5, grey at 3, red at 1–2; depleting stress is green below 3 and red above; promoting stress is green in the 3–4 band. **Missing data is never filled**: days without a record break the line instead of becoming zero, and a night marked only as "after midnight" becomes a red band rather than a guessed time. **Aggregation** runs by day, week, or month, defaulting to automatic (day up to 45 days, week up to 180, month beyond that) and stepping down to a finer grain when the data is concentrated; mean and median are switchable (scores default to median), bins with fewer than three days are drawn hollow, and every chart reports its coverage. **Interaction**: range (this week, last week, 30 days, 90 days, this year, all, custom), granularity, and statistic are all switchable; day types can be excluded while the excluded points stay visible in grey and out of the average; clicking any dot opens that day's record; the weight target (65 kg by default) is editable on the chart and the unit switches between kg and lb (each record stores its own unit, so mixed units are converted before plotting). Display settings live in `trend-view.json` and fall back field by field when it is damaged.
- **Log panel**: the "Log" button next to "插件内部数据" in the header views, filters, copies, and exports a complete timeline, and shows a red unread count when new warnings or errors appear. It filters by level (verbose / info / warn / error) and by source, searches by keyword, offers warnings-and-errors-only, and provides **copy all**, **download .txt / .json**, **clear**, and **self-test** (which writes one entry and really throws an uncaught exception to prove the pipeline works). Data reads and writes, backup rotation, read-back verification, SiYuan API calls with durations, uncaught exceptions, and the main user actions are all recorded automatically. Only warnings and errors are kept by default; when investigating, switch the relevant sources to "verbose" in the panel (the change is persisted in the plugin settings and applies immediately). Logs live in memory only (2000 entries by default, adjustable 200–10000), are **never written to disk, never sent over the network, and never contain note text** (content-bearing fields keep only length and type), and are cleared when the plugin unloads.
- Safe deletion that keeps descendants and clears references to the removed item.

- **A to-do list on Transactions and Ideas**: what is still left to do, kept deliberately separate from execution slices — a slice still means "one day of investment", while the to-do list is item-level and the two never lock each other. Three states: check = done (records the completion date), **✕ = dropped (stays in place, can be restored, it is not a delete)**; deleting is a separate action inside the expanded row. Progress reads "done x/y" and **dropped items do not enlarge the denominator**, matching the slice rule. Each row expands into notes/results and reference links (both accept pasted images); "Put into today" turns that item into today's execution slice, and "Multi-select" on the card header drops or schedules several at once. Type in the footer box and press Enter to add continuously — **pasting multiple lines creates multiple items in one go**.
- **Finishing a Transaction requires an explicit decision**: clicking "Mark complete" (or choosing the completed status) with unfinished slices or to-dos opens a confirmation layer that lists them and asks you to choose one of: drop everything and complete (slices become abandoned, to-dos get crossed out, all reversible), keep them as outstanding and complete anyway (one leftover note is written into Current State), or record why you are closing it now. With nothing unfinished the transaction completes directly, with no extra step. This is deliberately not a hard block: a slice marked missed or abandoned can never become completed again, so blocking would make any transaction that ever dropped a slice impossible to finish or abandon — leaving deletion as the only way out.
- **Current Action Details became structured fields** instead of one long block: **Current State** (the first thing to read on return — an automatic line with the facts the app already knows: slice progress, to-do progress, last update, days to the deadline, plus a hand-written line for "where I stopped / what is blocking me"), **Background & constraints**, **Prompt** (copyable with one click), **Staged results** (each entry dated, newest first), **Guidance & ideas**, and a collapsed **Definition of done**. Next Action stays a separate field but renders as the last field card, so "what is next" is written exactly once. Every field supports long-form editing in the large editor window with images and Markdown rendering; filled fields show a one-line preview and empty ones take a single line until expanded.
- **Action detail templates**: save the current structure as a template (field text only, no staged results) and apply it from the "Template" menu on any Transaction or Idea. Applying only fills **empty** fields, so nothing already written is ever overwritten — it doubles as "fill in the missing fields". Templates live in the plugin's own `action-templates.json` and are not tied to any single item.

## Storage and migration

Complete work items, sibling order, legacy per-date completion records, and execution slices live in the plugin-private `work-items.json`. Before every mutation Xingzhou rotates the previous version through three private backup files, saves the new store, then reads it back and verifies exact equality. An invalid primary store recovers from the newest valid backup; if none is valid, writes stop instead of silently overwriting data.

When upgrading from `0.4.x` with no internal store yet, Xingzhou reads the configured legacy Attribute View once, combines it with legacy plugin-private dependency data, and saves a migration snapshot. Normal operation no longer reads or modifies that Attribute View after migration.

Once the migrated work items have been verified in Xingzhou, deleting the legacy database or its containing document does not delete the internal work items. A work item may still retain a link to a SiYuan document; deleting that document only makes the link unavailable.

Life Rhythm uses a separate `daily-records.json`. It starts empty and never reads or migrates the old daily-data document. Checklist templates, display preference, and date-scoped completion state use `checklist.json`. Nutrition goals, food templates, and daily intake entries use `nutrition.json`. Work items, daily records, checklist configuration and progress, and nutrition records each use three rotating backups plus read-after-write verification. Every step is written to the log panel described above (revision, action, changed field names, which backup slot was rotated, and the read-back result), so a problem can be inspected by exporting that timeline. Logging is side-channel only: the revision rule, rotating backups, read-back verification, corruption recovery, and the stop-writing rule are all unchanged. The Trend view's display settings (selected metrics, range, granularity, statistic, filters, weight target and unit) live in `trend-view.json`: it is non-critical UI state, falls back field by field when damaged, takes no part in the revision and rotating-backup flow, and a failed write is reported without blocking any real data write. Life Rhythm's public read-only integration points are `getDailyRecordsSnapshot({ from?, to? })` and `getDailyRubrics()` for future weekly, monthly, or AI analysis. Trend definitions are centralised in the metric registry of `src/trend-metrics.ts`, where each metric declares its source field, unit, direction, bands, and reference lines, so adding one is a registration rather than a rendering change; `src/trend-chart.ts` turns a specification into geometry (ticks, bands, reference lines, gap-aware segments) as pure data that can be tested without a UI.

## Privacy

Xingzhou contains no remote service. Work items, daily records, nutrition records, the migration snapshot, and rotating backups use SiYuan's plugin-private data mechanism. Images in Current Action Details live in the SiYuan asset library (`data/assets/`); the plugin data stores only reference paths. Those images count toward your SiYuan cloud quota, and both cleanup and Storage Health only act on images referenced by Xingzhou, never on other assets in your notes. The plugin accesses local SiYuan content only for one-time legacy migration or when opening a linked document, and it never creates or deletes documents automatically.
